Specification

Which of these is genuinely computer vision?

Worth being precise before this is presented. Several capabilities on the wider list are platform events we simply aren't consuming yet — real value, fast to ship, but not our computer vision. Calling them CV invites one question — “so what model did you build?” — that the answer cannot survive.

CapabilityIs it CV?Why
Crack measurement Pure CV A width in millimetres exists only in pixels, calibrated against a scale reference. No sensor, PMS field or platform event can produce it. The strongest CV claim we have.
Standing water & condition Pure CV A water sensor gives a binary trip at one point on the floor. The extent, the location and which door rows sit in its path come only from pixels.
Overlock verification Pure CV + our data “Is a padlock physically on this hasp?” has no other source anywhere in the company. The PMS supplies delinquency; only a camera supplies the lock.
Vacant-unit-occupied Pure CV + our data Same door-state read as overlock. The PMS supplies “this should be empty”; the camera contradicts it.
Move-in / move-out record Pure CV A dated visual record of unit condition. Purely a pixels artefact, and the easiest one to explain to a customer.
Site condition score Pure CV Cleanliness, clutter, landscaping and signage are visual judgements with no structured equivalent.
Incident scene caption Partly CV The platform already classifies person and vehicle for free. Our CV adds the relational read — carrying boxes, moving away from the gate, gate closed. Real CV, but say what half is ours.
Camera tampering Not ours Platform event. Subscribe and route. High value, zero CV effort.
Loitering, intrusion, line crossing Not ours Platform events with configurable thresholds and zones.
Vehicle overstay, visit dwell Not ours Plate reads plus a native rule. No model involved on our side.
PPE compliance Not ours Helmet and vest already arrive in the person-attribute schema.
Break-in by sound Not CV Audio classification with decibels. Excellent signal — but audio, not vision.
Thermal anomaly Not CV Numeric temperature readings via a data schema. Sensor telemetry.
Traffic counting, camera health Not CV Counting analytics and device status events.
Say this today

“We have four computer vision use cases, and this prototype runs all four.”

  • Crack measurement — calibrated measurement from a technician photo
  • Standing water and condition — temporal change detection per camera
  • Overlock verification — presence check joined to delinquency
  • Incident scene captioning — relational scene reading on a live incident

All four are demonstrable in the prototype above, with the detection geometry computed rather than asserted. That is a defensible claim in front of a technical audience.

Don't say this

Don't fold tampering, loitering, PPE, audio or thermal into the CV story.

They are the strongest quick wins in the whole programme — but they belong to a different, equally good headline: “we already pay for roughly a dozen detection capabilities we don't yet consume, and switching them on is days of integration, not months of modelling.”

Two clean stories beat one inflated one. The integration story needs no precision number and ships faster; the CV story is where the defensible product sits. Presenting them separately means neither undermines the other under questioning.

The one-line version

CV is load-bearing wherever the answer exists only in pixels — a crack width, the extent of a puddle, whether a padlock is physically on a hasp, what a person in frame is actually doing. Wherever the platform already emits a structured event, our contribution is the join and the routing, not the vision. Both are worth funding; only the first is computer vision.

From prototype to production

The algorithm in this page is the real Stage 1 filter. What production adds is the model call, the data joins, and the places a finding can land.

Keep as-is

  • Frame differencing and thresholding
  • Connected-component region analysis
  • Minimum-region filter that rejects sensor noise
  • Crack measurement against a scale reference
  • The confirm / reject / unsure labelling loop

This is the cheap gate that keeps 85% of frames away from a paid model call.

Replace with the real thing

  • Procedural frames → Eagle Eye media endpoints
  • Templated caption → Vertex AI vision call, structured JSON out
  • Hard-coded context → BigQuery joins to PMS and sensor history
  • In-memory labels → append-only sd_vision.labels
  • Estimated cost → measured token usage per call

Add for production

  • Camera-to-door-row map (the real prerequisite)
  • Baseline frame index per camera and hour
  • Task creation via one authenticated mutation
  • Cloud Scheduler trigger, per-facility local time
  • Per-organisation spend quota and audit log

The honest limitation, restated

Scene four measures a crack because the lens is 40 cm from the wall and a scale card is in frame. A ceiling-mounted camera at twenty feet cannot do this — a hairline crack is one to three pixels there, infrared-lit at night. Fixed cameras do coarse, wide-area work: water, obstruction, door state, debris. Fine structural inspection needs a technician's phone, and that path reuses the same pipeline.
